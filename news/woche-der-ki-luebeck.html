Can a robot read handwriting? – Woche der KI Lübeck 2026

At the Woche der KI (AI Week) in Lübeck on 10 November, I demonstrated with the humanoid robot Pepper what I worked on in my bachelor's thesis at the Institute of Information Systems (IFIS), University of Lübeck: Pepper captures a handwritten note with its own camera, a vision-language model reads it, and Pepper reports the content by speech.

School students and many adults wrote their own notes and tried it out with Pepper. What impressed me most:

  • Many were fascinated by how well a robot can read handwriting today.
  • Some also found it unsettling. The uncanny valley effect was clearly noticeable at the booth.

And there were lots of great questions. Here are my answers to the most common ones:

Q: Is the robot meant to replace people?
A: No. It is meant to support them, for example with the tedious task of typing up handwritten documents. Control and decisions stay with humans.
Q: Why not just use a phone or a scanner?
A: A scanner or an app has to be operated, and you still have to read the result yourself. A robot that is already on site captures the sheet, processes it and answers by speech. This could help people who find reading or operating a device difficult.
Q: Why a humanoid robot?
A: Reading doesn't require a human shape. But a robot you can talk to and that responds at eye level is easier to approach for many people than a screen. At the same time, the uncanny valley effect shows that this very shape can also cause discomfort. A fascinating trade-off!
Q: How reliable is it, really?
A: That is exactly what we have studied systematically. The evaluation is part of my bachelor's thesis.

Many thanks to Thomas Sievers and Nele Rußwinkel for their supervision and support, and to everyone who stopped by!
